# Ranking production: breadth first, then refinement

## Latest owner correction — 13 September 2026

The owner rejected spending hours collecting sources and checking editions while publishing only 14 all-time books and four philosophy books. They want quite good research, completed quickly from established rankings and selections, followed by iteration. **Aim for approximately 250 entries in each broad all-time ranking**, especially all books and philosophy. This replaces the older 100–200-entry plan and the workflow that made detailed dossiers and images prerequisites for candidate inclusion.

The immediate deliverable is a broad, usable ranking in the app. More source collection is useful only when it improves that deliverable. Do not optimize for source counts, exhaustive commentary, or perfect metadata while the ranking remains tiny. Do not promise a particular minute count or pretend to have consulted sources that were not read.

## First pass

1. Inspect the existing database, ledgers and saved lists. Reuse relevant work already done. The five current targets already exceed the earlier 50-source minimum; do not spend another session collecting sources before expanding them.
2. Start with established published rankings, critics' polls, substantial editorial selections and useful aggregators. Extract their **full relevant candidate lists**, not a handful of titles mentioned in introductions. Supplement with a small targeted search for missing traditions and subjects. Consult non-English lists where useful; do not make exhaustive worldwide scholarship a first-pass prerequisite.
3. Build the comparison pool to at least the intended output size before deciding the top positions. Normalize authors, aliases, translations and multi-volume works in batches. Distinguish all-subject books, literary fiction, nonfiction, philosophy books and philosophers.
4. Check conspicuous omissions before publication. An available image or finished edition record must never be the reason The Doll receives a high all-time rank while major competitors are absent. Record editorial exclusions if any; do not force placements merely to echo the owner's example.
5. Compile an explained, source-backed first ordering of roughly 250 eligible entries. Preserve exact original source positions separately. Use source agreement and published ordering to inform the synthesis, with a brief explicit account of source dependence and scope. Do not manufacture numerical merit scores. An aggregator and its underlying lists are not independent votes; an unranked collection has no source merit positions; absence from a short list is not a last-place vote.
6. Retain both owner-selected views: critical standing/enduring influence and reading value today. The latter needs concise editorial reasoning where it differs; it is not simply an easy-reading list. A short inclusion/placement explanation and source links are enough for the first pass. Detailed candidate essays are later refinement.
7. Import substantial useful batches into the existing database with stable identities and revision history. Save source batches and import them incrementally as before. Update the queue and handover after each meaningful batch. Report entries actually saved, not only sources collected.

## Metadata and pictures

The owner still wants a picture for every book and author. Fetch covers and credited portraits efficiently in batches, reuse catalog images, and retain provenance. Keep a short retry queue for failed lookups. **Resolve missing media separately from intellectual selection**; do not shrink a 250-candidate ranking to the dozen records whose covers happened to download first. Temporary missing-image states must be explicit; a placeholder is not a verified portrait or cover and must not be reported as completion.

Verify work identity and English availability sufficiently for inclusion. Detailed translator comparisons, exact printing pagination and preferred-edition recommendations can follow the broad ranking. Leave unknown facts unset. Do not present an unverified edition as complete, silently substitute an abridgment, or use a different edition's cover as an exact match. Reading plans still need reliable edition page counts; ranking inclusion need not wait for every scheduling field.

The publication importer supports reviewed updates to populated rankings through `allow_expansion`, with an expected revision and explicit reasons for removed identities. Reviewed batches can set `allow_pending_metadata` so missing covers, portraits or verified editions do not block supported candidates. Retain honest per-entry metadata status, private-data protection, stable IDs and previous revisions. The earlier first-publication-only limitation is historical.

## Research depth and stopping

The earlier 50 distinct relevant consulted sources per completed researched target remains a counting floor, not a requirement for 50 sources per candidate. The later request for 100–200 sources was a breadth aspiration; it must not become a publication gate or an instruction to keep collecting indefinitely. Existing evidence already clears the floor for the five broad rankings. Count an aggregator as one consulted source unless its underlying lists were separately opened and examined; never claim that extracting 100 list references means consulting 100 sources.

Publish the broad first edition once identity, scope, source support and basic comparative ordering are coherent. Then iterate on disputed placements, underrepresented traditions, weak evidence, translations and media. Do not spend hours on one difficult image, marginal source or adjacent placement while hundreds of supported candidates remain unpublished. Preserve disagreement without turning every item into a research monograph.

## Sequence and preservation

First expand all books, literary fiction, nonfiction, philosophy books and philosophers. Then proceed to England, Ireland, USA, Germany, France, Poland, China and Japan, and philosophy by branch/topic. Smaller scopes need appropriate lengths rather than artificial padding.

Preserve the existing database, account, library, private weights/overrides, source positions and previous ranking versions. The current tiny revision-2 selections remain historical records, **not defensible global Top 14/Top 4 results**. Their ordinal positions must be reassessed against the broad pool, not treated as established ranks to preserve. Do not delete evidence or private records to start again.
