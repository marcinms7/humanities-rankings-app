## What changes, and why

- Kant moves from 3 to 25, while Hume moves from 5 to 1: approachable entry works change reading priority, not the historical claim.
- Ambedkar moves from 22 to 7, and Beauvoir from 13 to 4: contemporary questions receive more emphasis.
- Avicenna moves from 6 to 20: broad historical reception and a suitable first reading encounter are different judgments.

These movements are hypotheses made explicit for review. The current evidence does not establish that adjacent candidates are distinguishable with this precision.

## Outstanding comparisons

This is a reading-engagement ordering of people, not an average of book positions. Socrates needs treatment through mediated texts; Laozi and Zera Yacob need attribution-sensitive handling. Expand Ibn Rushd, al-Farabi, Ramanuja, Tsongkhapa, Dharmakirti, Nishida, Tanabe, Dussel, Oluwole, Oruka, Ramose, Astell, Conway, Sor Juana, Du Bois, and the many European challengers. Absence from the 25-person pilot is not exclusion from the intended 100–200.

Before publication: expand the candidate pool, strengthen thin dossiers, verify specific complete English editions, review work/collection identities, and develop the owner-controlled criteria and weights. Keep these draft positions separate from every publisher’s original order and from personal overrides.
